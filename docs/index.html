About this map

DNL (Day-Night Average Sound Level) is the 24-hour average A-weighted sound level with a 10 dB penalty added to everything between 22:00 and 07:00. It is the metric used by the US FAA and many other regulators to describe airport noise exposure.

This map is built from ADS-B Exchange position reports covering one day. Each aircraft sighting is turned into an A-weighted level at every nearby ground cell (roughly 1 km) and integrated over each day. When several days are combined, every layer is the per-day average: DNL of the mean sound energy, and mean seconds per day for the other two. ADS-B Exchange publishes a full day of data for the first of each month, so multi-day builds are made of those.

Time above 45 dB is for people who care about audible events rather than averages: it is the clock time per day during which at least one aircraft is estimated at 45 dB or louder at that cell, roughly the level that is clearly audible indoors in a quiet room. Overlapping aircraft count once, so it never exceeds 24 hours. Aircraft only: road and rail noise are not included, and a blank cell can mean no receiver coverage rather than silence.

Main assumptions:

  • Source levels come from a table indexed by ADS-B emitter category (light, small, large, heavy, rotorcraft...) and flight phase, not from the specific aircraft type.
  • Spherical spreading, 1 dB/km atmospheric absorption, and SAE AIR 5662 lateral attenuation by lateral distance and elevation angle.
  • Height above ground from ETOPO 2022 terrain (2 arc-minute), with airport elevations overriding it near runways.
  • Night is defined by local solar time (UTC + longitude / 15 h), not civil time zones.
  • Traffic density counts seconds of aircraft presence per cell per day; overlapping aircraft add.

These are estimates, not measurements. Coverage depends on ADS-B receivers, so oceans and remote areas are under-reported, and a handful of days may not reflect typical operations.
